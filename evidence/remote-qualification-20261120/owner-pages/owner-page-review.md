(7) Preserve the approved 17:00 glyphs and apply the 909/808 R22 radius and type. (8) Port the same system to website and firmware together.

## Owners and acceptance boundaries

- **Sound and firmware runtime** supply versioned SuperJ6 and Synth One IDs, domains, formatters and observations. SuperJ6 requires separate layer waves, PWM width and enable, continuous SUB and three LFO rates with Hz/tempo semantics. The runtime owner also supplies musical step/phase and activity or meter observers. **Remote** binds those admitted sources and verifies the resulting controls and displays.
- **Firmware/MIDI protocol** owns stock DNR2 admission, matched ports, versioned parameter/control and typed project/pattern/sample transactions. **Remote** owns browser read/review/apply and controller feedback against that contract. Emulator lifecycle, stock host identity and TouchOSC note round trips each have narrower scope. Physical USB/BLE remains with the authorized device-test owner.
- **Remote** owns Draft editing contracts, viewport density, control reachability and any additional step/song/playlist semantics supported by the model. Actions that cue, mutate content or change the instrument need a corresponding owner-supplied contract.
- **Firmware UI** owns the original 17:00 glyph comparison set and firmware R22 consumption. **Web** owns site destinations and matching components. **Remote** compares its consumer to the shared source, and **Integration** qualifies the three lanes together on exact revisions.
- **Independent test-and-merge** owns the exact-head gate result. Historical screenshot targets without approved baselines are captures. The four grid-continuity assertions and one historical header comparison assess their individual criteria. This per-page review identifies observed differences without assigning an overall fidelity score.
