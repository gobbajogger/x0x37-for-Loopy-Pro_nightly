A closer look at X0X.
Firmware layouts, reconstructed in vector.

    
Versions & original screen references
Vector reconstructions preserve the 240 × 240 firmware layout, RGB565 colors, labels, control positions and shown states, using production drawing commands and original Barlow font outlines. Curves and lettering are smoothly rendered rather than enlarged from pixels. These are synthetic host-rendered states, not device photographs.
HOME and MIX reconstruct the shown states from reference source 54f2ffbc. SMC shows the native PR168 render from 65a0588, a newer source preview outside the downloadable builds. This source includes the drum-label and Pause-lock edits; the frame shows the 909 sequencer, not the lock state. Its firmware link failed the size limit by 108 bytes, so this is not downloadable or validated firmware. Downloadable firmware versions and safety status come from the current catalog in Install firmware; illustration source versions do not select or qualify a package. Make it part of your setup.

    
Play it on its own, add a hands-on mixer, or bring your loops into a Loopy Pro performance.

    

      
Play standaloneWrite a complete groove on the keyboard.
909 and 808 drums, two acid bass parts and sliced breaks play together. Make patterns, record knob moves, then chain or arrange them into a song.
Get going: install the matching model, then press PLAY. Use the pads for steps and pattern changes; select a part and turn its sound controls. No computer is needed for playing.

      
Add an SMC mixerKeep both hands on levels and sound changes.
Eight faders and encoders control the main mix or individual drum voices. Mute, momentary solo and record controls make live changes quick. Fader pickup waits for the saved position before changing the sound.
Connect: use a package with Bluetooth included. On the keyboard, open GLOBAL → BLUETOOTH MIDI, select BT ROLE → SMC HOST, enable Bluetooth and restart. Match SMC PRESET to the mixer's DAW or USER preset.
One direct SMC Bluetooth connection at a time. This uses Bluetooth MIDI, not Bluetooth audio. The newer controller-screen design shown above is a source preview, separate from downloadable firmware.

      
Perform with Loopy ProLaunch clips and keep your hands on the music.
The keyboard works as an APC40 mkII-style clip controller: pads launch clips, colors follow Loopy feedback, and PLAY / REC control playback and recording in clip mode. The keybed plays MIDI instruments in Loopy through SCALE, CHORD and ARP modes.
Connect: connect the keyboard to your iPad or Mac by USB, select its APC40 mkII controller in Loopy Pro, then press KNOB BANK to enter or leave clip mode. OCT− / OCT+ move the local pad view. Change the page in Loopy to reach clips outside its controller box.
Use USB for Loopy when Bluetooth is connected directly to SMC; the keyboard uses one Bluetooth role at a time. These NIGHTLY packages remain hardware-untested.

    

    
What can you do with it?

    

      
Create and play sounds Drums, acid bass and breaks.
  • 909 / 808: choose from 11 drum voices in each kit, edit their sounds and sequence each voice.
  • Two 303 parts: make separate bass lines with accents, slides and sound shaping; play live with Free Play. Pitch and mod wheels add temporary tune and cutoff movement on the selected 303.
  • TB-3PO: generate and mutate acid lines using density, accents, slides, root and scale—useful when you want a starting point to reshape.
  • Break player: slice a loop into eight pieces, change their sequence and use rolls, fills and retriggers for variation.

      
Sequence and record Build variations without stopping the groove.
  • 32 patterns, up to 64 steps per part: give parts different lengths and rates; each can use its own pattern.
  • Live or step recording: tap drum hits, enter 303 notes or record while playing.
  • Motion and per-step locks: record knob movement or hold a parameter value on one step for changing timbre and rhythm.
  • Probability and swing: let selected steps vary between passes and shift the groove away from straight timing.
  • Chains and Song: chain patterns for a jam or arrange bars with pattern choices and mutes.

      
Perform and mix Shape a groove as it plays.
  • Hands-on levels: keyboard fader banks cover the five parts, reverb, delay and the master filter, with pickup for smooth handovers.
  • Sound movement: use the performance controls, part mutes, pan and sends; a shared reverb, tape delay and master compressor bring the parts together.
  • SMC control: expand the surface with eight strips and drum banks. The available firmware and newer UI preview are identified separately.
  • Loopy clip mode: switch pads from local sequencing to the host's clip grid; return to normal X0X operation with KNOB BANK.
  • SCALE / CHORD / ARP for Loopy: keep played notes in a scale, build chords with inversions and strum, or create arpeggios with rate, gate, swing, humanize and latch.

      
Use your own loops and keep your work Make the setup sound like you.
  • Sample management: prepare your own loops, inspect available slots, then explicitly send them to the keyboard. Sample memory is limited; the tool shows what fits.
  • Backup and restore: save sample slots to a file and restore them through Manage samples.
  • Project save / load: keep the current sounds, patterns and song; autosave and undo / redo support editing. Sample backup is a separate step.
  • USB MIDI: use note and clock connections with other apps; choose one clock source for a steady tempo.

      
Check the fit for your gear Compatibility and practical limits.
The current catalog offers SMK-37 Elite and Pro packages. Starrykey 37 Play uses the Pro model; choose the name intended for your keyboard. The keyboard's pads and keys play with fixed velocity. SMC uses a single direct Bluetooth MIDI link; Loopy uses USB in the combined setup. No Bluetooth audio or direct multi-SMC connection is offered here.
First installation replaces the previous firmware's working data. Save anything you need before switching. A matching stock firmware package can be restored with the original updater; see Restore original firmware.
